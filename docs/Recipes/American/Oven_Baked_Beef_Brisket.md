## Oven-baked beef brisket temperatures, cooking times, marinades, and techniques:  

Beef brisket is a tough, collagen-heavy cut from the breast area of the cow. To make it melt-in-your-mouth tender in a standard home oven, it requires low heat, ample time, and proper moisture-sealing techniques.

**Oven Temperature & Cooking Times:**

- **Standard Low & Slow (250°F / 120°C)**: The benchmark Texas-style method. Cook for **1 to 1.5 hours per pound** (approx. 4–6 hours for a 4–5 lb brisket flat; 8–12 hours for a full 10–12 lb packer cut).
- **Ultra Low Slow-Roast (200°F / 93°C)**: Roast covered for roughly **2 hours per pound**.
- **Target Internal Temperature**: **200°F to 205°F** at the thickest section (a thermometer probe should glide through like soft butter).

**Marinades & Seasonings:**

- **Liquid Marinade**: Combine soy sauce, beef consommé/broth, lemon juice or red wine vinegar, garlic, and liquid smoke. Marinate covered in the refrigerator for 12 to 24 hours (or up to 48 hours) to break down muscle fibers and infuse deep smoky flavor.
- **Dry Rub Option**: Apply a rub of coarse kosher salt, coarse black pepper, garlic powder, brown sugar, and smoked paprika generously before baking.

**Key Preparation & Baking Techniques:**

1. **Fat Cap Up**: Place the brisket fat side up in a roasting pan or rimmed baking sheet so the fat continuously renders down into the meat as it bakes.
2. **Double Foil Wrap**: Wrap the brisket tightly in heavy-duty aluminum foil (or butcher paper) to trap steam and break down tough connective tissues.
3. **Rest & Slice Against the Grain**: Rest the brisket wrapped in foil for **at least 30 to 60 minutes** before carving. Always slice thinly **against the grain** (perpendicular to the muscle fibers) for maximum tenderness.
