### Convection oven adjustments for low-and-slow meats:  

Convection ovens use a fan to continuously circulate hot air around the oven cavity, which transfers heat more efficiently than conventional static baking. For low-and-slow BBQ cuts like ribs and brisket, here is how to adjust:

## General Conversion Rules:  

1. **Reduce Temperature by 25°F (15°C)**: The golden rule for convection is to lower the oven target temperature by 25°F (for example, if a standard recipe calls for 275°F, set your convection oven to **250°F**). Maintaining a lower temperature ensures that collagen has enough time to melt into gelatin without the outer layers drying out.
2. **Alternative — Reduce Time by 20% to 25%**: If your oven does not automatically adjust the temperature and you leave it at the conventional setting, check for doneness 20% to 25% earlier than the recipe specifies.

## Key Tips for Ribs & Brisket:  

- **When Wrapped in Foil**: While the meat is sealed tightly in aluminum foil, convection air cannot touch it directly. However, the oven environment stays more consistently hot. Sticking to the **25°F temperature drop** (keeping the cooking duration about the same) yields the best fall-off-the-bone tenderness.
- **Uncovered / Saucing Phase**: Convection works exceptionally well during the final 10–15 minutes when you unwrap the meat to brush on BBQ sauce or set a crust. The fan accelerates browning and caramelization, so monitor it closely so the sugars in the sauce don't burn.
