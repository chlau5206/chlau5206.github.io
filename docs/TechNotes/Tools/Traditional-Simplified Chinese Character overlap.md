## Commonly Used Characters (~3,500–6,500)

**~70% of commonly used Traditional characters are identical in Simplified Chinese.** Only about 2,235 characters were actually simplified (out of ~8,105 in the official _通用规范汉字表_). So roughly **30% of high-frequency characters** have a different form.

## Full Character Corpus (~50,000+)

If you compare the entire historical corpus, the overlap is much higher — **~95–96%**, since the ~2,235 simplified forms represent only a tiny fraction of all attested characters.

## Bottom Line

For practical, everyday purposes: **about 7 out of 10 common characters look exactly the same** in both systems. The simplification was deliberately concentrated on high-frequency characters (which is why a Simplified-only reader can already recognize roughly half of a Traditional text on first encounter).


**Chinese** is not a single language but a family of mutually unintelligible varieties, officially classified into **ten main dialect groups** (or seven, depending on the linguistic framework). These varieties share a common written system but differ significantly in pronunciation, vocabulary, and grammar.

The **ten primary dialect groups** are:

1. **Mandarin** (65.7% of speakers): The official standard (_Putonghua_), spoken in northern and southwestern China.
    
2. **Min** (6.20%): Spoken in Fujian, Taiwan, and Southeast Asia; includes **Hokkien** and **Fuzhounese**.
    
3. **Wu** (6.10%): Spoken in Shanghai, Zhejiang, and southern Jiangsu; includes **Shanghainese**.
    
4. **Yue** (5.60%): Spoken in Guangdong, Hong Kong, and Macau; includes **Cantonese**.
    
5. **Jin** (5.20%): Spoken in Shanxi and Inner Mongolia.
    
6. **Gan** (3.90%): Spoken primarily in Jiangxi province.
    
7. **Hakka** (3.50%): Spoken by scattered communities across southern China and overseas.
    
8. **Xiang** (3.00%): Spoken in Hunan province.
    
9. **Huizhou** (0.30%): Spoken in southern Anhui.
    
10. **Pinghua** (0.60%): Spoken in Guangxi.
    

While **Mandarin** and **Cantonese** are the most widely spoken, many varieties like **Wu** and **Min** are not mutually intelligible with Mandarin or each other. Linguists estimate there are over **300 identifiable dialects**, with more than 1,000 if village-level variations are included.
