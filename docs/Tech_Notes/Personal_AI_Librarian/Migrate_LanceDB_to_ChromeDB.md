==**AnythingLLM comes with a private, built-in vector database powered by LanceDB by default**==

1. ChromaDB: The "Developer First" Database

ChromaDB's primary goal is extreme simplicity and rapid prototyping. It handles a massive amount of boilerplate code for you automatically. [[1](https://zilliz.com/comparison/chroma-vs-lancedb), [2](https://kanopylabs.com/blog/lancedb-vs-chroma-vs-sqlite-vec)]

- **The Good:** It has a "batteries-included" design. Unlike LanceDB, Chroma can accept raw text documents and automatically turn them into embeddings internally using a built-in default framework, taking less than 10 lines of Python code to set up. [[1](https://kanopylabs.com/blog/lancedb-vs-chroma-vs-sqlite-vec)]
- **The Catch:** Because its index relies heavily on HNSW algorithms stored directly in your system memory, it eats up a lot of RAM as your database grows. On your 16GB laptop, if you upload massive, thousands-of-pages document libraries, Chroma can start fiercely competing for memory space against your active LLM. [[1](https://kanopylabs.com/blog/lancedb-vs-chroma-vs-sqlite-vec), [2](https://www.actian.com/blog/developer/comparing-embedded-vector-databases/)]

2. LanceDB: The "Performance Lakehouse" Database

LanceDB was written in Rust with a laser focus on low resource overhead, extreme speed, and rich media. [[1](https://medium.com/@patricklenert/vector-databases-lance-vs-chroma-cc8d124372e9), [2](https://zilliz.com/comparison/chroma-vs-lancedb)]

- **The Good:** Because it reads data straight from your disk layout using high-performance columnar scanning (benchmarking up to 100x faster than Parquet files), **it uses almost no RAM**. It will keep your 16GB system incredibly lean. Furthermore, because it natively speaks Apache Arrow, it can easily handle the raw image extractions generated by visual models like your **Qwen3-VL-4B**. [[1](https://enterprisedna.co/directories/compare/chromadb-vs-lancedb), [2](https://www.actian.com/blog/developer/comparing-embedded-vector-databases/), [3](https://suhasbhairav.com/blog/lancedb-vs-chroma-columnar-local-vector-storage-vs-simple-developer-rag-prototyping)]
- **The Catch:** It requires you to pass pre-computed vector matrices explicitly (it doesn't auto-embed text by default), and its ecosystem documentation, while growing rapidly, is slightly more developer-centric. [[1](https://kanopylabs.com/blog/lancedb-vs-chroma-vs-sqlite-vec)]
Which should you choose for AnythingLLM?

Because AnythingLLM handles all the background configurations for you automatically, your choice depends on what your AI-Librarian is indexing:

- **Choose ChromaDB** if you are importing your text-heavy **Obsidian Vault markdown notes** and want a bulletproof setup that has deep community tracking and instant, out-of-the-box text indexing.
- **Choose LanceDB** if you plan to throw **heavy PDFs filled with diagrams, screenshots, or textbooks** at your system. LanceDB’s disk-centric architecture won't consume your precious laptop memory, leaving the maximum amount of your 16GB RAM wide open for your running **Gemma 4 12B** model. [[1](https://www.actian.com/blog/developer/comparing-embedded-vector-databases/), [2](https://www.respan.ai/market-map/compare/chroma-vs-lancedb), [3](https://kanopylabs.com/blog/lancedb-vs-chroma-vs-sqlite-vec), [4](https://suhasbhairav.com/blog/lancedb-vs-chroma-columnar-local-vector-storage-vs-simple-developer-rag-prototyping)]

==**LanceDB and ChromaDB are not compatible, and AnythingLLM does not support automated data migration between them.

The Re-Embedding Migration Workflow

To migrate your data, you must manually re-index your files. Fortunately, AnythingLLM stores a local copy of your raw files in its internal storage cache, meaning you do not need to re-gather your PDFs or notes from scratch. [[1](https://docs.anythingllm.com/setup/vector-database-configuration/overview), [2](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLM/comments/1plo7er/anythingllm_how_to_export_embeddings_to_another_pc/)]

The exact manual migration steps include:

1. Open your workspace's document manager and click the **Delete** icon next to your uploaded files to clear out the disconnected LanceDB pointers. [[1](https://docs.anythingllm.com/setup/vector-database-configuration/overview)]
2. Go to **Settings > Vector Database** and switch the provider from the built-in LanceDB to **Chroma**. Enter your local Chroma server credentials. [[1](https://docs.anythingllm.com/setup/vector-database-configuration/overview), [2](https://docs.useanything.com/setup/vector-database-configuration/local/chroma)]
3. Return to your workspace, select the files from your local storage cache, and click **Save and Embed**. [[1](https://docs.anythingllm.com/setup/vector-database-configuration/cloud/chromacloud)]
